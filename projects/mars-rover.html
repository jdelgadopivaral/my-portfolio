Mechanical Design · In Progress

Mars Rover Sample Cache System — HURC

Developing a simplified drilling, sample-separation, and cache system for HURC’s University Rover Challenge rover while working with the science team to translate sampling requirements into a practical mechanical design.

Project Goal

As one of the mechanical engineering leads supporting HURC’s science team, I am helping develop the rover system used to collect and store soil samples. The system must collect the science sample from the first 10 cm of soil, collect a separate sample from deeper material, and retain approximately 5 g in a removable cache.

Simplifying the Previous Design

I was tasked with simplifying the previous sampling system to reduce unnecessary mechanical complexity and make the design easier to build, integrate, and operate.

The current design focuses on accomplishing the required sampling tasks with fewer moving components and a clearer material path.

Current System Design

The current design uses an auger to collect soil and move material upward through the drilling system. The mechanism extends downward to reach the required sampling depth while keeping the overall assembly compact enough for rover integration.

The same drilling system is intended to support both the shallow science sample and the deeper cache sample.

Sample Separation & Cache

The system must keep samples from different depths separated. In the current concept, material from the first 10 cm is used as the science sample; once the auger reaches deeper material, a funnel-style ramp redirects it toward the cache.

The cache is designed to hold approximately 5 g. After collection, a servo-driven closure is intended to shut the cache and retain the material. The cache will also be removable so the stored sample can be retrieved after the rover task.

Current Status

The current CAD establishes the simplified auger, sample-routing, and servo-closed cache concepts. I am currently testing and refining the geometry while working with the science team and waiting for approval of the current design direction before moving into physical prototyping and rover integration.
